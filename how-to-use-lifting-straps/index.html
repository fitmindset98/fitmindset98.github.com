How to Use Lifting Straps: The Lasso Wrap, Step by Step

How to put on and wrap lasso lifting straps correctly, tighten them on the bar, and avoid the common mistakes that make them slip.

Short answer

Make a loop with the strap, slide your hand through so the tail comes out on the thumb side, then pass the tail under the bar from the far side and back over the top. Roll the bar to wind the strap tight, close your grip over it, and keep squeezing as you lift.

Lasso straps look simple, but most people fumble with them the first few times: wrong hand, wrong direction, or a wrap that slips loose as soon as the bar leaves the floor. Once you get the method down, setting up takes about five seconds per hand.

What you need

A pair of lasso straps, the most common type: a strip of cotton or nylon with a loop sewn into one end. These instructions are for that style. Figure 8 straps work differently and are mainly used for very heavy deadlifts and shrugs.

Step 1: Make the loop

Feed the loose end of the strap through the sewn loop to make a lasso. Lay it flat with the sewn loop facing you.

Step 2: Put it on the right way

Slide your hand through the lasso so it sits around your wrist, just above the wrist bone. The tail should come out on the thumb side of your hand and run across your palm.

If the tail comes out on the pinky side, the loop is set up for the other hand. Take it off, flip it over, and try again. Getting this right is what makes the strap lie flat across your palm.

Snug the loop so it's firm but not cutting off circulation.

Step 3: Wrap the bar

  1. Put your hand on the bar where you normally grip, with the tail hanging over the bar.
  2. Pass the tail under the bar from the far side, then bring it up toward you and over the top.
  3. Hold the end against the bar with your fingers.

One full wrap is usually enough. Long straps can go around one and a half or two times for a more secure hold.

Step 4: Tighten it

With your fingers pinning the tail, roll the bar a quarter or half turn in the direction that winds the strap tighter (usually toward you). You'll feel the strap pull your hand snug against the bar. Then close your grip over the top of everything.

Do the same with the other hand. With a loaded barbell, it often helps to strap one hand, then roll the bar slightly with that hand while you wrap the second one.

Step 5: Lift

Keep gripping. Straps take load off your grip, they don't replace it. A loose hand inside a strap puts all the pressure on your wrist and can let the bar shift.

Common mistakes

MistakeResultFix
Tail on the pinky sideStrap twists and won't lie flatFlip the loop for that hand
Wrapping over the bar firstStrap loosens as you pullGo under from the far side, then over
Skipping the tightening rollSlack in the strap, hand slidesRoll the bar until it's snug
Loop too low on the handStrap digs into the base of the thumbMove the loop up to your wrist
Relaxing your gripAll the load hangs on your wristsSqueeze the bar as usual

Using straps on dumbbells and cables

The method is the same. On dumbbells, wrap the handle and roll it in your hand to tighten before you lift it off the rack. On cable rows and pulldowns, wrap the handle or bar while it's still supported, then take the weight.

Straps are especially useful on rows, pulldowns and shrugs, where your back can handle more than your hands. FitBesidesHealth's back workout for men is a good place to put them to work.

Taking them off

After the set, open your hand and unroll the bar, and the strap will release. Leave the lasso loosely on your wrist between sets so you don't have to rebuild it every time.

Questions lifters ask

Which way do lifting straps go?

The tail should come out on the thumb side of your hand and run across your palm. If it comes out on the pinky side, flip the loop.

How many times should you wrap a lifting strap?

One full wrap is usually enough. Longer straps can go one and a half or two times for extra security.

Should I still grip the bar with straps?

Yes. Straps take load off your grip, but you should still squeeze the bar.
