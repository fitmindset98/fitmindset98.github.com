How to Brace With a Lifting Belt (Step by Step)

Step-by-step instructions for bracing into a lifting belt, a drill to learn it, and the mistakes that waste a belt's support.

Short answer

Get into position, take a big breath down into your belly and sides, push that pressure out into the belt in every direction, then tighten your abs on top of it. Hold the breath and tension through the hardest part of the rep, and reset before the next one.

A belt is only as good as the brace you put into it. Plenty of lifters wear one for years without getting much from it, because they suck their stomach in, breathe into their chest, or let the air out halfway through the rep. Here's how to do it properly.

What bracing actually means

Bracing is taking a big breath into your trunk and then tightening the muscles around it, so your midsection becomes a stiff, pressurized cylinder. Think "push out in every direction", not "pull in".

The belt gives that pressure something firm to push against all the way around: front, sides and back. That's why belted lifts often feel more solid, and why a belt does very little if you don't push into it.

Step-by-step: bracing with a belt

  1. Set the belt. Fasten it so you can just slide a flat hand between belt and stomach after a normal exhale. Our guide on how tight a lifting belt should be covers this in detail.
  2. Get into position first. Set your feet and grip before the big breath. For squats, that means under the bar and walked out. For deadlifts, that means at the bar with your grip set.
  3. Breathe low. Take a big breath through your nose or mouth and send it down into your belly, sides and lower back. Your shoulders shouldn't rise much.
  4. Push into the belt. Expand against the belt in every direction, as if you were trying to push it off. You should feel firm pressure at the front and sides.
  5. Lock it in. Tighten your abs on top of that pressure, like you're about to take a punch, and hold your breath.
  6. Lift and hold. Keep the air and the tension through the whole rep, including the hardest point.
  7. Reset between reps. Breathe out at the top, take a fresh breath, and brace again before the next rep.

A drill to learn it without a belt

Lie on your back with your knees bent. Put your thumbs on your lower back and your fingers on the sides of your waist, just above your hips. Breathe in so your fingers get pushed out to the sides and your thumbs feel pressure at the back. Then tighten your abs without losing that expansion.

Once that feels natural, do the same thing standing, then with goblet squats, then under the bar. Five minutes of this before your workout pays off quickly.

Common bracing mistakes

MistakeWhat happensFix
Sucking the stomach inNothing presses into the belt, so you lose supportThink "push out", not "hollow"
Breathing into the chestShoulders rise, pressure sits too highHand on belly, make it move first
Breathing out at the sticking pointTrunk softens exactly when you need it mostHold the air until you're past the hardest part
Belt too tightYou can't get a full breath inLoosen one hole
Bracing before getting setYou run out of air before the rep startsPosition first, breathe last

Bracing for squats vs deadlifts

The principle is identical, but the timing differs. For squats, you breathe and brace at the top before every rep. For deadlifts, many lifters breathe at the top, bend down to the bar while holding it, then pull. Others breathe at the bottom. Both work, as long as the brace is set before the bar leaves the floor.

Bracing is part of good technique, not a substitute for it. If your squat depth or bar path is off, FitBesidesHealth's guide to squat form is worth reviewing before you add load.

A safety note

Holding your breath under heavy load (the Valsalva maneuver) briefly raises blood pressure considerably. That's normal for healthy lifters, but if you have high blood pressure, a heart condition, or a history of hernias or eye problems such as glaucoma, talk to your doctor before training with heavy, breath-held sets.

Questions lifters ask

Should I suck in my stomach when wearing a belt?

No. Sucking in leaves nothing pressing into the belt. You want to push your belly and sides out against it.

When should I breathe during a squat?

Breathe and brace at the top before each rep, hold through the descent and the hardest part of the way up, then breathe out at the top.

Is holding your breath while lifting safe?

For healthy lifters it's normal. If you have high blood pressure, heart problems, hernias or eye conditions such as glaucoma, talk to your doctor first.
