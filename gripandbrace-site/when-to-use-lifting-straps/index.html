When to Use Lifting Straps (and When Not To)

A lift-by-lift guide to lifting straps: when they help you train harder, when they hold you back, and how to keep your grip strong.

Short answer

Use straps when your grip gives out before the muscle you're training: heavy shrugs, Romanian deadlifts, heavy rows and high-rep deadlifts. Skip them on warm-ups, in your first months of lifting, and on heavy deadlifts if you compete in powerlifting.

Lifting straps are one of the cheapest pieces of gear you can buy, and one of the most argued about. Use them everywhere and your grip may lag behind. Never use them and your back, hamstrings and traps can end up undertrained because your hands quit first. The answer is in between.

What straps do

Straps wrap around your wrists and the bar, so part of the load hangs on the strap rather than your fingers. Your grip still works, just not as the limiting factor. That lets you keep a set going until the muscle you're actually training is tired.

The one rule that matters

Use straps when grip is stopping a set before the target muscle is done. If your hands are opening while your back or hamstrings still have reps left, straps are doing their job. If your grip isn't the problem, you don't need them.

When not to use straps

  • In your first months of training. Your grip grows quickly early on, and the weights you're using are rarely heavy enough to need help.
  • On warm-up sets. Warm-ups are free grip training.
  • If you compete in powerlifting. Straps aren't allowed in competition, so your heavy deadlifts need to be trained with the grip you'll use on the platform.
  • As a fix for a slipping grip caused by sweaty hands or poor technique. Try chalk and a better grip position first.

A simple plan for deadlifts

  1. Pull your warm-ups and first working sets double overhand, without straps, for as long as you can hold on.
  2. Switch to a mixed grip or hook grip for heavier sets if you need to.
  3. Add straps for top sets or higher-rep back-off sets only when your grip is failing before your back and legs.

Grip technique is only part of a good deadlift. If your setup or bar path needs work, FitBesidesHealth's deadlift form guide breaks it down step by step.

Keep training your grip

Using straps on your heaviest sets is fine as long as you also train your grip directly. Two or three sets of farmer's carries, dead hangs or heavy holds once or twice a week will keep your hands from becoming the weak link.

Which straps to get

For most lifters, a pair of simple cotton or nylon lasso straps is all you need. They're cheap, quick to use and work on almost everything. If you've never used them, start with our guide on how to use lifting straps. If you want to browse, you can compare lasso lifting straps.

Questions lifters ask

Are lifting straps cheating?

No. They're a tool for training your back, hamstrings and traps harder when grip is the limiting factor. Just keep training your grip separately.

Do straps weaken your grip?

Only if you use them for everything. Using them on your heaviest sets and training grip directly keeps your hands strong.

Are straps allowed in powerlifting?

No. Straps aren't permitted in competition deadlifts, so powerlifters should train their heavy pulls without them.
