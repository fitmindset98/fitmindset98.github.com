10mm vs 13mm Lifting Belt: Does Thickness Matter?

How 10mm and 13mm lifting belts differ in stiffness, comfort and break-in time, and which one most lifters should buy.

Short answer

For most lifters, 10mm is the better choice. It's already stiff enough to brace against, breaks in faster and is more comfortable. A 13mm belt is the competition maximum and mainly suits powerlifters and very heavy squatters.

Three millimeters doesn't sound like much. On a leather lifting belt, it's the difference between a belt you can wear comfortably on day one and one that takes weeks to stop fighting you.

The rules that set the limits

Most belt designs trace back to powerlifting rules. The International Powerlifting Federation allows belts up to 10cm wide and 13mm thick, which is why 13mm is the "maximum" you'll see advertised. The full equipment rules are in the technical rulebook published on the IPF website, along with a list of approved belt brands.

So a 13mm belt is the thickest legal option for competition. A 10mm belt is the more common everyday choice.

Does the thicker belt help you lift more?

There's no solid research showing a 13mm belt lets you lift more than a 10mm belt. Both are already rigid enough to give your abs something firm to push against, and that's where the benefit comes from.

Some very strong lifters prefer 13mm because it feels more solid under extreme loads. That's a reasonable personal preference, but for most people the extra stiffness is beyond what they need.

Who should buy 10mm

  • You're buying your first belt.
  • You train squats, deadlifts and presses in a general strength or hypertrophy program.
  • You have a shorter torso, or you find thick belts jam into your ribs and hips.
  • You want a belt that feels good within a week, not a month.

Who should consider 13mm

  • You compete in powerlifting and want the maximum the rules allow.
  • Your squat is very heavy and you've worn a 10mm belt long enough to know you want more rigidity.
  • You're willing to put up with a long break-in period.

A useful benchmark: if your lifts aren't yet past intermediate on FitBesidesHealth's strength standards by body weight, a 13mm belt is unlikely to make any difference you'll notice.

Width matters more than thickness

Before you worry about 10mm vs 13mm, make sure the belt is the same width all the way around. Tapered belts, wide at the back and narrow at the front, give much less support where you actually brace. A 10mm uniform 4-inch belt beats a 13mm tapered one for squats and deadlifts.

Breaking in a new belt

  • Wear it for warm-up sets and lighter work for the first few sessions.
  • Roll it tightly in both directions, then leave it rolled overnight.
  • Don't soak it or bake it. Shortcuts like that can weaken the leather and stitching.

Questions lifters ask

Is a 13mm belt IPF legal?

Yes. IPF rules allow belts up to 13mm thick and 10cm wide, so 13mm is the thickest legal option.

Will a 13mm belt let me lift more?

There's no good evidence it adds more than a 10mm belt for most people. Both are rigid enough to give your brace something to push against.

How long does a 13mm belt take to break in?

Usually several weeks of regular use, compared with a few sessions for a 10mm belt.
